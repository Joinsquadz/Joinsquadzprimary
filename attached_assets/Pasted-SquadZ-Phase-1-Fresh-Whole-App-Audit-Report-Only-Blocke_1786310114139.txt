SquadZ — Phase 1: Fresh Whole-App Audit (Report-Only, Blocker vs. Enhancement Split)
Context: significant work has landed since the last whole-app triage audit — four concurrency fixes (#575, #592, #593, #599), event-chat migration to pagination, media backup to R2 with restore verification, and monitoring. This is a fresh audit of the CURRENT state to catch anything changed or missed, and — critically — to cleanly separate genuine launch blockers from nice-to-have enhancements so launch scope stays controlled. Report-only: do NOT fix or build anything in this pass.
The most important output: a clean two-bucket categorization
Every finding must be labeled as exactly one of:
	•	BLOCKER — would make the app broken, unsafe, non-compliant (app store policy / legal), or non-functional for real users. Must be fixed before submitting to stores.
	•	ENHANCEMENT — makes a working app better, but the app is shippable without it. Deferred to post-launch unless trivially cheap.
Be honest and disciplined about this split. The temptation is to label things BLOCKER to be safe — resist it. A genuinely shippable-without-it item is an ENHANCEMENT even if it’d be nice. When truly ambiguous, label it AMBIGUOUS and explain the tradeoff so the founder decides.
What to examine
Cover the full app, with fresh eyes on the current state:
	1.	Every user-facing feature — squads, plans/events/trips, RSVPs, itineraries, ideas, availability polling, cost splitting, feed, moments, vault, DMs, packing lists, invites, report/block, calendar export, conflict detection, profile/account, paywall/entitlement. For each: does it actually work end-to-end, are there broken states, dead ends, or confusing flows?
	2.	The recently-changed areas specifically (higher risk of new issues): the migrated event chat, the concurrency-fixed paths (membership, cost, vote, poll conversion), the backup system. Confirm these are working correctly in their new form, not just that they were built.
	3.	Launch-compliance items (these are where real BLOCKERS often hide):
	•	Age gating / minimum age enforcement (was flagged as absent earlier — is #577 done, or still open?)
	•	Terms of Service / EULA presence
	•	Privacy policy completeness
	•	Any content-moderation gaps that app store review would flag for a UGC social app
	•	Any place user data could leak across privacy boundaries
	4.	Error handling and failure states — does the app handle backend errors, empty states, network failures gracefully, or are there raw errors / crashes / silent failures that would look broken to a real user? (Note: #576 mobile resilience may address some of this — confirm status.)
	5.	Anything structurally concerning that doesn’t fit above — surface it, labeled appropriately.
Ground rules
	•	Report-only. No fixes, no enhancements, no cost-splitting (#583) work.
	•	Use the [CODE]/[DB]/[TEST-EXISTS]/[INFER] evidence labeling from the prior triage audit — distinguish what you verified from what you inferred.
	•	Do NOT re-report the concurrency issues already fixed (#575/#592/#593/#599) as open — confirm they’re resolved and move on.
	•	If the app is too large to cover exhaustively in one run, prioritize BLOCKER-hunting in the compliance, recently-changed, and error-handling areas first, and clearly note what wasn’t reached.
Output
	1.	A categorized findings list: every item labeled BLOCKER / ENHANCEMENT / AMBIGUOUS with a one-line rationale.
	2.	A short “launch-blocker summary” at the top — just the BLOCKERs, so the must-fix-before-submit list is immediately visible.
	3.	The ENHANCEMENT items collected separately as a post-launch backlog.
	4.	Confirmation of what’s genuinely solid and needs no work (so it’s clear what’s healthy, not just what’s flagged).
