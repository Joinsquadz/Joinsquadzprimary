UX Craft Sweep: Flow-by-Flow Analysis + Enhancement
Goal
Raise SquadZ’s execution quality to the level of the best consumer social
apps (Instagram, Facebook, Twitter/X, Partiful, Airbnb) — their polish,
speed, and interaction craft, not their features. SquadZ stays private,
squad-only, and intentional: no feeds-for-engagement, no public profiles, no
discovery mechanics, nothing that posts without explicit user intent. The
bar is: a user coming from Instagram should feel the same level of care in
every tap, transition, and screen — while the product remains a coordination
tool for friend groups.
Process — two phases, do not skip Phase 1
Phase 1: Flow-by-flow analysis (report first, no code changes)
Walk every core flow end to end as a first-time user AND as a returning
power user, and score each against best-in-class consumer apps. For each
flow, report: what’s clunky, what’s slow, what’s confusing, what’s missing
feedback, and what a top-tier app would do differently at that moment.
Flows to analyze (all of them):
	1.	First-run: install/open → signup → onboarding → landing in the app
with zero squads. Is there a wow moment? Does the empty app sell what
SquadZ does, or is it a dead end?
	2.	Squad creation + invite: create → name → invite friends → waiting
for them to join. What does the inviter see while waiting? Is inviting
delightful or a chore?
	3.	Invite acceptance (the other side): receiving a link → preview →
signup → landing inside the squad. This is the #1 growth-critical flow —
every second of friction here costs members.
	4.	Event creation: how many taps from intent to created? What can be
deferred/optional vs. what blocks? Compare against Partiful’s creation
flow specifically — they set the bar for lightweight event creation.
	5.	Trip creation + itinerary building: adding stops, proposing vs.
direct-add, the voting experience. Is building an itinerary together
fun, or does it feel like data entry?
	6.	RSVP + attending experience: from notification/link → viewing →
responding → what happens after. Including the conflict-detection
warning moment.
	7.	Voting on proposed stops: casting a vote, seeing others’ votes,
resolution. Does this feel social and alive, or like a form?
	8.	Packing list: shared check-off, assignment. Quick interaction check.
	9.	Day-of experience: what does the app offer on the day of an event or
during a trip? (This is likely a gap — most planning apps go silent at
the moment of highest engagement.)
	10.	Post-event: what happens after a plan ends? Photos, memories, the
Past tab. Is there any reason to come back?
	11.	Upgrade/paywall moments: hitting the 5-plan or 2-squad cap,
Squadz+ purchase. Do these feel fair and well-timed, or punitive?
	12.	Returning-user home: opening the app with active squads and plans.
Is the most relevant thing (next upcoming plan, pending votes, unread
activity) surfaced instantly, or does the user have to dig?
For each flow, grade A–F against best-in-class and list specific,
implementable improvements ranked by impact. Deliver this as a report before
touching any code.
Phase 2: Implement (after report review)
Once the report is reviewed and priorities confirmed, implement approved
items. Expected categories of improvement — use judgment per flow:
Motion & feedback
	•	Screen transitions (no hard cuts between screens)
	•	Press states on every tappable element
	•	Haptics on meaningful actions (RSVP confirm, vote cast, check-off,
stop confirmed)
	•	Optimistic UI on lightweight actions (check-off, vote) — instant visual
response, reconcile with server after
	•	Loading skeletons instead of spinners for content screens
	•	Celebratory moments where earned: squad fully RSVP’d, trip confirmed,
first squad created — subtle, not confetti-spam
Hierarchy & scannability
	•	Returning-user home leads with the single most relevant thing (next plan,
pending vote), not a uniform list
	•	Visual grouping in Trips·Events·Past as plans accumulate
	•	Consistent card anatomy: date, squad, status readable at a glance
	•	Typography discipline: Syne for display, Inter for body, deliberate scale
Reducing friction
	•	Cut taps from every creation flow; defer optional fields; smart defaults
(next weekend, most-recent squad)
	•	Inline editing wherever possible instead of navigate-to-edit screens
	•	Invite acceptance flow: minimize steps between tapping a link and being
inside the squad
Perceived performance
	•	Instant screen loads via cached/stale-while-revalidate data
	•	Prefetch likely-next screens (squad → its plans)
	•	No blank screens ever — skeleton, cached data, or empty state
Empty states as onboarding
	•	Every empty state teaches and prompts the next action (zero squads,
zero plans, empty itinerary, empty packing list)
	•	The zero-squad first-run state is the most important screen in the app
for conversion — treat it as a landing page, not an absence of content
Craft details
	•	Icon consistency (single icon set, single stroke weight)
	•	Spacing system applied uniformly
	•	Dark-theme depth: elevation/borders so surfaces read as layered, not flat
	•	Brand gradient used deliberately for primary actions and moments of
delight, not scattered
Hard constraints — do not violate
	•	No public content, no discovery feeds, no follower mechanics, no public
profiles, no algorithmic ranking of content
	•	Nothing posts or shares without explicit user intent
	•	All content and permissions continue to inherit from the squad — no new
audience pickers
	•	Freemium gates stay as-is (5 plans/year, 2 squads, Squadz+ gating) — this
sweep improves how they’re presented, not what they gate
	•	Brand system stays canonical: Syne/Inter, #0F0F14 background,
#FF6B2C→#FFB23E gradient
	•	No new heavyweight dependencies without flagging first (e.g. don’t pull in
a maps SDK, video processing, or an animation library >100kb without
asking)
Regression guard
After Phase 2 changes, re-run the standing 10-user simulation from the July
5 audit and confirm all previously-passing checks still pass. Craft changes
must not break function.
